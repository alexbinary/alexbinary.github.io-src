Since 2019, I've been running an [online parts store](https://store.bricklink.com/alexbinary) on the BrickLink platform. Customers can order individual parts from several thousand unique references. Good stock organization is therefore critical. Initially, I used an off-the-shelf solution that helped me get started but eventually showed its limitations. I then undertook to create a completely custom storage solution.

{:style="margin-top: 3rem;"}
### Papi Max StackX Drawers: Advantages and Disadvantages

*Papi Max StackX Drawers* is a product specifically designed for LEGO enthusiasts, promising high storage density.
The system consists of individual modules composed of an external case in which a drawer slides.
The cases are available in white or black and can be stacked and assembled together.
The drawers are transparent and equipped with a handle at the front.
Up to four dividers can be added widthwise and two lengthwise to form up to 15 compartments.

#### The 👍

<ul class="plus">
<li>Sold as individual elements, allowing to form a global structure of desired size and shape</li>
<li>Adjustable internal layout</li>
<li>Ability to completely remove a drawer to carry it to the work area</li>
<li>Drawers can slide out almost completely allowing easy access to the entire surface</li>
</ul>

#### The 👎

<ul class="minus">
<li>Impossible to extract an individual compartment to bring it to the work area or empty its contents. This makes moving pieces from one compartment to another particularly tedious</li>
<li>Reconfiguring the dividers requires completely emptying the drawer</li>
<li>The dividers rest by gravity and are not locked.
With drawer manipulation, they lift up and allow pieces to leak into neighboring compartments</li>
<li>The drawer often gets stuck or blocks if not handled with care</li>
<li>The transparent plastic of the drawers is prone to yellowing in sunlight</li>
<li>High price</li>
</ul>

Eventually, the removable divider system was appealing on paper but impractical in use.
Added to that, the low build quality and high price were prohibitive to continue with this solution.

### A Custom DIY Solution

My solution relies on a system of modular boxes that can be carried, moved, and arranged freely in large drawers. The drawer opening is smooth, effortless, and gives a quality impression.

Development proceeded through progressive exploration.
I started by developing the boxes then the drawers to store them.

### Box Development

During 2021, I had the opportunity to learn laser cutting at a shared workshop near my home.
After a training session, I started experimenting with this machine, and I quickly grasped its potential for making boxes that could serve as the basis for a custom storage solution.

After quickly exploring different box assembly and construction techniques, I settled on a <b>finger joint</b> assembly.

I based the size of the first boxes on the compartment size of the storage system I was using at the time, which was a 75mm square box.
A box with these dimensions can accommodate a LEGO piece 8 units long with just enough margin.
For the height, I wanted to respect the LEGO brick ratio, considering my box was equivalent to a 2x2 brick.
LEGO experts know that 2 LEGO units correspond to 5 plate heights, and a brick height is 3 plates high.
For a 75mm side box, this gives a height of 75*3/5 = 45mm.
I found this too high, so I went down to 2 plates instead of 3, or 30mm.
I find this is the perfect ratio.

From there, assuming my base box is 2x2 units wide and 2 plates high, I experimented with all sorts of variants in 1x1, 1x2, 2x3, 2x4, 3x3, etc. in versions 1, 2, 3, or 4 plates high. I quickly realized that large size and low height models are useful as trays for containing pieces during construction, for example.
I made bigger ones, which I still commonly use today.

### Furniture Development

When I started to have a certain number of loose boxes, the need for a higher level of storage became apparent. I then progressively developed drawer furniture to store the boxes. I experimented with different designs and manufacturing techniques, and I now have a set of 5 pieces of furniture that I use daily. Some were made with recycled materials.

The quality feel when opening and closing the drawers was an important criterion for me.
I wanted my drawers to give an impression of solidity and quality,
with smooth opening and closing,
without tilting as the drawer opens,
and without fear of dislodging the drawer if opened too quickly.
I also wanted the drawers to open to full depth to allow access even to boxes at the back.

After finding and ordering full-extension metal slides on Amazon,
I made a first furniture prototype with 10mm poplar plywood.
I cut the pieces with a jigsaw but the result was very average.
Nothing was really straight, and the opening/closing of drawers wasn't smooth
and open drawers would collide.

The shared workshop that hosts the laser cutting also has a woodworking shop.
After completing the necessary training,
I undertook a second prototype, this time using the table circular saw.
The result was significantly better.

I then stepped up by creating a furniture piece twice as wide, then a piece with 16 drawers, including one double height for more voluminous pieces.
To prevent the bottom from sagging, I added a partition that divides the drawer into two parts and helps support the bottom.

## Assessment and Perspective

I now have a total of 5 pieces of furniture of different capacities that I use daily to store my LEGO pieces for my online store.

I still need more storage space,
and I plan to make more furniture pieces,
always seeking to improve the design.
Drawer positioning is still not ideal without exploding the machining time budget.

Also, the current slides don't allow the drawer to extend beyond its depth,
which still makes access to boxes at the back difficult,
especially when there's the handle of the drawer above just overhead.

For future furniture pieces, I'd like to try adding a large illustration that spans across the drawer fronts.

I'm also thinking about making modules to insert into IKEA-type Kallax shelves.
